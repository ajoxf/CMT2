# Overseas brokers open China futures to NRIs

**For an individual Non-Resident Indian (NRI), the Overseas Intermediary (OI) route is the only practical way into Chinese futures. QFI is a licence for institutions only. On the narrow list of "internationalised" contracts the two routes cost about the same at exchange level: both use the same exchange fee schedules and the same exchange margins. They differ in commission layers, currency handling and QFI's fixed setup costs.** Exchange margins look low in the rulebooks (a 5% minimum), but in 2026 they mostly ran between **8% and 15%**, and they rise before every Chinese holiday and as delivery approaches. Brokers add their own margin on top. Neither route publishes commission, custody or QFI setup costs, so those must be quoted by each broker. India's own rules matter more than China's. An NRI or OCI who is genuinely non-resident under FEMA and trades with money earned abroad faces no Indian bar. An Indian resident cannot use the Liberalised Remittance Scheme (LRS) to fund margin on any overseas exchange, Chinese ones included. Indian companies may only hedge real commodity exposure through their bank. Funds and brokers based in GIFT City IFSC can in principle serve non-resident clients, but no live China offering was found. The brokers confirmed as OIs are mostly in Singapore and Hong Kong: Orient Futures Singapore, Marex, StoneX, Phillip Nova, Straits Financial, CITIC Futures International, CN First and the Nanhua group. BANDS Financial is a direct INE participant. Besides Trading Technologies (TT), the platforms with documented China connectivity are CQG, Bloomberg, FlexTrade and Horizon. All of them reach the exchanges through the mainland trading systems CTP and Esunny. Stellar's China coverage could not be confirmed.

> **Read this first.** This report is general information, not legal, tax or investment advice. Many primary sources could not be fetched in this research session because of network restrictions. These included the exchange websites (SHFE, INE, DCE, GFEX), RBI, Orient Futures Singapore, CQG, FIA and several Indian law firms. Many findings therefore rest on search-engine snippets of those pages rather than the full documents. Items marked *unverified* should be checked against primary texts. Get advice from a FEMA specialist, a tax adviser and the broker itself before you act.

## Same exchange costs, different broker layers: OI vs QFI

The two routes reach different sets of products. The OI route covers only the "internationalised" (also called "specified") contracts. In March 2025 BANDS Financial counted **24 futures and options** on INE, DCE and ZCE ([BANDS, Mar 2025](https://bands.financial/files/BANDS%20Webinar%20-%20Trading%20Commodity%20Futures%20in%20China.pdf)). Orient Futures' 2026 overview lists soybean, palm olein, PTA, rapeseed, PET resin, SC crude, TSR20, bonded copper, nickel and lithium carbonate, together with their options ([Orient Futures SG](https://www.orientfutures.com.sg/china-market-access/china-internationalised-futures-options-2026/)). On **3 July 2026** GFEX opened lithium carbonate futures and options to overseas traders, the exchange's first internationalised product ([China Daily](http://nansha.guangdong.chinadaily.com.cn/2026-07/07/c_1195718.htm); [Caixin](https://www.caixinglobal.com/2026-07-05/china-opens-lithium-futures-market-to-foreign-traders-102460689.html)).

QFI covers far more. Orient Futures says that after the QFII/RQFII scheme was merged into QFI in March 2025, QFIs could trade **38 commodity futures, 30 commodity options, 4 index futures and 3 index options** ([Orient Futures](https://www.orientfutures.com.sg/china-market-access/china-expands-qfi-access-global-traders/), broker claim, unverified). A law firm put the figure at **115 contracts by May 2026** ([YaoWang Law](https://www.yaowanglaw.com/newsinfo/11250795.html)). ETF options were added from 9 October 2025 and CGB treasury futures from April 2026, both for hedging only ([SSE](https://english.sse.com.cn/access/qfiirqfii/scope/); [Caixin](https://www.caixinglobal.com/2026-04-24/china-opens-government-bond-futures-to-foreign-investors-for-hedging-102437743.html)). QFI is also the only way into CFFEX ([Orient Futures](https://www.orientfutures.com.sg/china-market-access/internationalised-vs-qfi-china-futures-access/)). The exact product counts conflict between sources, and no official list dated November 2026 could be retrieved.

**Exchange fees are the same on both routes.** They are set contract by contract in exchange notices that apply to every participant, and no separate foreign or OI fee schedule was found. One example: from the 24 June 2026 night session, INE charged **RMB 20 per lot on SC2608 crude**, with intraday close-outs free ([Mondo Visione](https://mondovisione.com/media-and-resources/news/shanghai-international-energy-exchange-ine-has-released-its-notice-on-adjusting-2026623/)). **Exchange margins are also the same on both routes.** The rulebook minimum is typically 5% ([INE handbook](https://www.ine.cn/eng/market/futures/energy/sc/manual/202004/W020250806318691873930.pdf)). In practice exchanges set higher working margins:

- **DCE iron ore:** margins step up to 10% and then 20% as delivery approaches ([DCE manual](http://www.dce.com.cn/dceg/file/2025-05-25/1748165227597ff8080819701ddb31019706c4744d21b2.pdf)).
- **ZCE PTA:** 12% in March 2026, and up to 15% for the 2026 Labor Day holiday ([Futu](https://news.futunn.com/en/post/69846370/the-zhengzhou-commodity-exchange-zce-issued-a-notice-adjusting-the); [SMM](https://news.metal.com/newscontent/103877378-ZCE-Adjusting-Trading-Margin-Standards-and-Price-Limit-Ranges-for-Certain-Futures-Contracts-During-the-2026-Labor-Day-Ho)).
- **GFEX lithium carbonate:** 13% for speculators from January 2026 ([Devdiscourse/Reuters](https://www.devdiscourse.com/article/education/3774925-guangzhou-futures-exchange-tightens-lithium-carbonate-trading-rules)).

The real cost differences come from three things:

- **Commission layers.** An OI does not trade on the exchange itself. It authorises a mainland futures-company member to trade for it, and settlement runs through that member ([INE](https://www.ine.cn/eng/services/members/become/)). An OI that belongs to a mainland futures group, such as Orient Futures SG, probably charges one combined markup. An independent OI that routes through a third-party mainland broker probably adds a second layer. Both points are inferences, not sourced figures.
- **Currency handling.** OI clients can post **USD as margin, with a 5% haircut at INE** ([INE handbook](https://www.ine.cn/eng/market/futures/energy/sc/manual/202004/W020250806318691873930.pdf)), and reportedly at GFEX too ([Discovery Alert](https://discoveryalert.com/lithium-futures-china-gfex-overseas-traders-carbonate-2026/), unverified). Profits, losses and fees are still settled in RMB, so some currency conversion is unavoidable.
- **QFI's fixed overhead.** A QFI needs a CSRC licence filed through an onshore custodian bank, SAFE registration and an onshore futures account ([Orient Futures](https://www.orientfutures.com.sg/china-market-access/china-qfi-scheme-5-things/)). Investment quotas were abolished in 2019, and since 27 October 2025 a CSRC "one-stop" process lets applicants file the licence, FX registration and account opening together ([Chambers](https://chambers.com/articles/csrc-unveils-work-plan-to-optimize-the-qfi-regime)). Repatriation is free, has no lock-up period, and can be made in foreign currency or RMB ([CSRC](https://www.csrc.gov.cn/csrc_en/c102030/c1370479/content.shtml)).

| Dimension | OI route (internationalised products) | QFI route |
|---|---|---|
| Who can use it | Overseas institutions and individuals who pass suitability checks, through an OI licensed in its home jurisdiction | Licensed institutions only; individuals cannot use it |
| Product scope | About 24 (Mar 2025) to about 35 (2026, unverified) commodity futures and options; no CFFEX | About 115 contracts on all six exchanges incl. CFFEX; index, ETF-option and CGB products are hedging-only |
| Exchange fees | Exchange schedule (e.g. SC2608 at RMB 20/lot) | Same |
| Exchange margin | 5% rulebook minimum; 8–15% typical in 2026; higher before holidays and delivery | Same |
| Broker add-on margin | Set by each broker; **not sourced** | Set by each broker; **not sourced** |
| Commissions | OI markup, plus the mainland broker's if the OI is unaffiliated; **no figures found** | Mainland broker only; **no figures found** |
| Custody and setup | Broker account opening; margin held in the mainland broker's segregated margin account | Onshore custodian is mandatory; **custodian fees, legal costs, timeline and any minimum AUM not found** |
| Margin currency | RMB or USD (5% haircut at INE); settlement in RMB | RMB, funded onshore through the custodian |
| Physical delivery | Bonded delivery possible on internationalised contracts | QFIs excluded from physical delivery ([Dacheng](https://shanghai-en.dacheng.com/Party_2/992.html)) |

For a single trader focused on commodities, the OI route is cheaper and quicker. It needs no custodian, no licence and no onshore fixed costs. It also lets the trader post USD as margin. QFI is worth its overhead only for an institution that needs CFFEX, the wider commodity list or onshore hedging, and that can spread the custodian and compliance costs across a large portfolio. **I could not source commission rates, custody fees or QFI setup costs from any broker or custodian, and I have not estimated them.** Ask Orient Futures SG, Marex, StoneX, Phillip Nova and others for written quotes, and compare them on four points: per-lot commission, add-on margin, the FX spread on USD margin, and data and platform fees.

## Frequent rule changes, test-gated onboarding and programme-trading filings slow foreign traders

**Access is legal but not frictionless.** INE's eligibility rules require individual clients to pass a knowledge test in person. Institutional clients' authorised traders must do the same. Clients also need at least 10 days of simulated trading or equivalent real trading records, although brokers can waive some items ([INE Eligibility Rules](https://www.ine.cn/en/regulation/regulation/911319599.html); [INE 2019 rules](https://www.ine.com.cn/upload/20190530/1559211977341.pdf)). Every client gets its own trading code, so a broker cannot pool clients anonymously in a single omnibus account. Exchanges also add up the positions of accounts under common control when they apply limits ([SHFE actual-control rules](https://www.shfe.com.cn/eng/services/Rules/SHFERules/202508/t20250807_828559.html)). A client that breaches an intraday opening limit can be barred from opening new positions for **at least three trading days** ([SHFE abnormal-trading rules](https://www.shfe.com.cn/eng/services/Rules/SHFERules/202508/t20250807_828558.html)). Exchanges also impose hard daily caps on opening positions. GFEX, for example, capped LC2601 lithium carbonate at **500 lots per day** in November 2025 ([Devdiscourse/Reuters](https://www.devdiscourse.com/article/education/3774925-guangzhou-futures-exchange-tightens-lithium-carbonate-trading-rules)).

Systematic and high-frequency traders face extra rules. The CSRC's Futures Programme Trading Measures took effect on **9 October 2025**. Every programme trader must file through its broker before trading. High-frequency firms must also disclose their strategy types, peak order-cancellation rates, server locations and contingency plans ([Chambers](https://chambers.com/articles/csrc-released-the-provisions-on-futures-program-trading)). Exchanges can charge high-frequency traders differentiated fees ([INE programme-trading rules](https://www.shfe.com.cn/eng/services/Rules/INERules/202508/t20250814_828669.html)). Brokers may not rebate fees to traders flagged as high-frequency ([Yicai](https://www.yicaiglobal.com/news/chinas-futures-exchanges-wont-lower-high-frequency-trading-fees)). Caixin reported that after rebates were removed in 2024, some foreign high-frequency firms considered moving to markets such as India ([Caixin](https://www.caixinglobal.com/2024-08-22/in-depth-chinas-tightening-rules-put-foreign-high-frequency-futures-traders-in-limbo-102228776.html)). Colocation is described as "less and less accessible" to foreign firms ([YaoWang Law](https://yaowanglaw.com/newsinfo/8443537.html)).

China's Futures and Derivatives Law, in force since 1 August 2022, also reaches conduct outside China that disrupts the domestic market. That can expose offshore swap structures or lookalike trading to Chinese enforcement ([Hankun](https://hankunlaw.com/upload/newsAndInsights/e1b7d0974e1378c822d800d50534d929.pdf)).

**Delivery and day-to-day market risks are equally real:**

- **Delivery.** Non-bonded delivery needs a Chinese VAT special invoice, which a foreign entity without Chinese tax registration cannot issue ([SHFE delivery rules](https://tsite.shfe.com.cn/eng/services/rules/shfe/911403538.html); [Lexology/HFW](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=d28e9dc5-e3f9-41f2-9a27-9d9bcd5a3395)). In practice, foreigners should close domestic-only contracts before the delivery month.
- **Abrupt rule changes.** Margins, price limits and fees change at short notice before holidays and in volatile markets, and notices usually appear in Chinese first. The FIA notes that the authorities have intervened readily in the past ([FIA](https://www.fia.org/marketvoice/articles/china-further-expands-international-access-its-commodity-markets)).
- **Wider risks.** The US State Department's 2026 Investment Climate Statement cites capital controls, low policy predictability and sanctions risk linked to China's ties with Russia and Iran ([State Dept 2026](https://www.state.gov/reports/2026-investment-climate-statements/china)). In April 2026 testimony to the USCC, Johannes Petry said global institutional participation remains limited ([USCC](https://www.uscc.gov/sites/default/files/2026-04/Johannes_Petry_Testimony.pdf)).

There is one important tax advantage. China's Ministry of Finance extended the **individual income tax exemption for overseas individuals trading internationalised commodity futures to 31 December 2027** ([Shanghai government tax FAQ](https://english.shanghai.gov.cn/en-Tax2/20250814/5f5b66c137cb4484b27843f7b33f8206.html); [China Daily, Aug 2023](https://www.chinaservicesinfo.com/s/202308/25/WS64e80d72498ed2d7b7e993c1/ministry-releases-preferential-tax-policies-for-investors.html)). Corporate income tax and VAT treatment of institutional futures gains outside this exemption could not be confirmed.

## Indian residency status, not China's rules, decides who can trade

No Chinese rule singles out Indian nationals. The internationalised route is open to "overseas entities and individual investors" that meet requirements on capital, expertise, operations, governance and experience ([Mondaq/WilmerHale](https://www.mondaq.com/china/Finance-and-Banking/812600/China-Moves-To-Open-Commodity-Futures-Trading-To-Foreign-Participants)). India's Press Note 3 restricts investment *into* India from countries that share a land border with it. It does not govern Indians investing in China, and it was partly eased in March 2026 ([Carnegie](https://carnegieendowment.org/research/2026/04/indias-press-note-3-gamble-opening-the-fdi-door-to-china)). What decides the answer is the investor's status under India's Foreign Exchange Management Act (FEMA).

| Investor type | Position from the Indian side |
|---|---|
| Resident individual using LRS remittances | **Prohibited in practice.** RBI's FAQs exclude "remittance from India for margins or margin calls to overseas exchanges / overseas counterparty" ([RBI FAQ 146](https://rbi.org.in/Scripts/FAQView.aspx?Id=146); [RBI FAQ 115](https://rbi.org.in/SCRIPTs/FAQView.aspx?Id=115)). The 2022 Overseas Investment rules leave derivatives and commodities out of permitted portfolio investment unless RBI permits them ([Mondaq](https://webiis08.mondaq.com/pdf/1224738.pdf)). |
| NRI or OCI living abroad, using money earned abroad | **Allowed from India's side.** LRS applies only to people resident in India. The rules of the country of residence and the broker's suitability checks apply instead. |
| NRI using NRE or NRO money | **Allowed with documentation.** Move the money abroad first. NRO repatriation is capped at **USD 1 million per financial year** after tax, with Forms 15CA/15CB ([Citibank India](https://online.citibank.co.in/citi-nri/transfers-from-india-repat.htm)). |
| Returning NRI (resident under FEMA; may be RNOR for tax) | **Grey.** FEMA s.6(4) protects foreign assets acquired while non-resident ([TradingQnA summary](https://tradingqna.com/t/trading-derivatives-in-usa-from-india/20239/363)). Whether it covers opening *new* futures positions is unsettled. |
| Indian company hedging real commodity exposure | **Hedging only,** through an authorised dealer bank under RBI's 2022 Commodity and Freight Hedging Directions, which apply to "residents other than Individuals" ([Taxmann](https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/rbi-issues-master-directions-on-hedging-of-commodity-price-and-freight-risk-in-overseas-markets)). Whether any Chinese exchange counts as a permitted venue is unverified. |
| Indian mutual fund, AIF or PMS trading for return | **Effectively prohibited.** Mutual funds may use overseas exchange-traded derivatives only to hedge or rebalance positions whose underlying is a security, within a USD 7bn industry cap ([Vested](https://vestedfinance.com/in/globed/accessing-global-markets/global-investing-through-domestic-funds-and-etfs/sebi-limits-on-overseas-investment-by-mutual-funds/)). |
| GIFT City IFSC broker or fund | **Possible in principle for non-resident clients and IFSC funds.** IFSCA's Global Access framework of 12 August 2025 limits resident Indian clients to products permitted under LRS ([KS&K](https://ksandk.com/investment/ifsca-revamps-ifsc-global-access-framework-for-2025/); [ConventusLaw](https://conventuslaw.com/report/india-global-access-providers-in-ifsc-gift-city-a-gateway-to-global-markets/)). **No IFSC entity offering China futures, and none registered as a Chinese OI, was found.** |

FEMA residency depends partly on intent. Someone who leaves India for employment becomes non-resident even before the 182-day count is met ([Deutsche Bank India](https://www.deutsche.bank.in/en/nri-banking/non-resident-connect/fema-residential-status.html)). FEMA residency can also differ from residency under the Income-tax Act ([Rashmin Sanghvi](https://rashminsanghvi.com/downloads/taxation/international-taxation/Residence-under-FEMA-and-Income-tax.html)). This creates real traps for NRIs:

- **Long stays in India.** An NRI who spends extended time in India can become FEMA-resident partway through a year, which brings the LRS and Overseas Investment restrictions back into force.
- **Funding from India.** Paying margin directly from Indian accounts or cards breaches the rules.
- **Gifts from resident relatives.** Gifting LRS money to an NRI relative who then trades is likely to be seen as getting around the rules. This is an inference; no source addresses it directly.
- **Brokers that rely on self-declaration.** Some offshore brokers onboard Indian residents on a "non-resident" self-declaration alone, which industry commentary flags as a compliance risk ([Finance Magnates](https://www.financemagnates.com/forex/the-non-resident-mirage-how-brokers-turn-india-s-rules-into-opportunity/)).

The enforcement risk is real. India's Enforcement Directorate has frozen or attached tens of crores of rupees in its OctaFX actions from 2022 to 2024 ([Business Today](https://businesstoday.in/latest/trends/story/ed-freezes-rs-214-crore-of-forex-trading-app-octafx-348564-2022-09-29); [ED](https://enforcementdirectorate.gov.in/sites/default/files/latestnews/Press%20Release%20OctaFx20.04.2023.pdf)). Those cases targeted platforms, not individual futures traders, but they show that FEMA penalties are applied in practice.

On Indian tax, a true NRI's offshore gains are generally outside Indian tax, and with China's exemption in place, Chinese tax should be nil. A resident who is ordinarily resident (ROR) is taxed on worldwide income. Foreign accounts must be disclosed in Schedule FA. Under the Black Money Act, failing to disclose carries a flat **Rs 10 lakh** penalty, with relief below Rs 20 lakh for assets other than property from 1 October 2024 ([Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/personal-finance/news/story/relief-from-penalty-for-small-value-assets-under-the-black-money-law-is-it-adequate-444164-2024-09-03)). Paying the tax does not cure a FEMA breach.

## Beyond TT and Stellar: CQG, Bloomberg, FlexTrade and Horizon reach China via CTP and Esunny

Every foreign platform reaches the Chinese exchanges in the same way. Orders pass through the platform's China-hosted gateway into a mainland futures-company member's order-routing system and then to the exchange. That routing system is usually **SFIT's CTP** (run by an SHFE subsidiary) or **Esunny** (a wholly owned ZCE subsidiary) ([CQG CTP help](https://help.cqg.com/cqgic/25/Documents/comprehensivetransactionplatformctp.htm); [CME Esunny](https://www.cmegroup.com/partner-services/esunny-international.html)). In practice, a platform supports China only where a partner broker supports it.

## Conclusion

The OI route turns the choice between OI and QFI into a choice between product list and overhead, not between cheap and expensive trading. QFI does not lower exchange fees or margins. It widens what can be traded and adds an onshore custodian, so it suits only institutions that need CFFEX or contracts outside the internationalised list. For Indian investors the decisive question is FEMA status, not which broker to use. An NRI whose non-residence is well documented can open an account with a Singapore OI today. The same person who returns to India, or who funds margin from India, moves into prohibited or grey territory. The most underused option is an Indian corporate with real iron ore, palm oil or crude exposure hedging on DCE or INE through its bank. RBI's directions allow this in principle, but no live example was found, and it is worth raising with an authorised dealer bank.

Three things to watch. First, China's individual income tax exemption for overseas futures traders ends on 31 December 2027 unless renewed. Second, GIFT City's Global Access framework could become a legitimate channel for non-resident Indians if an IFSC broker registers as an OI or partners with one. Third, the products and exchanges open to foreigners keep expanding (SHFE in 2025, GFEX in 2026, CGB futures for QFIs), so any product list goes stale within months. Before committing capital, get written quotes on commission, add-on margin and FX spread from at least two OIs. Confirm exchange-level OI status on the official exchange lists, and get a FEMA opinion for any case short of a clear non-resident trading with money earned abroad.
