Research method note: about 22 web searches and fetches. These hosts failed DNS resolution from this session and could not be fetched: www.shfe.com.cn, www.ine.cn, www.cqg.com, plus orientfutures.com.sg (reported by the coordinator). ## Brokers: Who offers foreign investors access to Chinese onshore futures?

### Takeaway
Foreign access runs through four routes. The most common is the Overseas Intermediary (OI) route, where a foreign broker registers with each exchange separately and routes orders through a mainland futures-company member. The others are INE's Overseas Special Brokerage Participant (OSBP) status, direct accounts at mainland futures companies, and QFI for the full onshore universe. Confirmed OIs include Orient Futures Singapore (all five commodity exchanges), StoneX, Marex, Phillip Nova, Straits Financial, CN First International, CITIC Futures International and Nanhua-group entities, with BANDS Financial as an OSBP. Access is overwhelmingly institutional. I found no broker serving Indian residents or NRIs for China futures, and no GIFT City route.

### Cited Findings

**Access framework**
- INE crude oil can be accessed through four channels: (1) a domestic futures company that is an INE member, (2) a qualified INE foreign brokerage institution (OSBP), (3) becoming a direct foreign trader, or (4) an Overseas Intermediary. A foreign investor must get a unique INE trading code before trading. — [Clifford Chance, Mar 2018](https://www.cliffordchance.com/content/dam/cliffordchance/briefings/2018/03/a-milestone-development-for-the-china-futures-market-crude-oil-futures-trading-on-the-shanghai-international-energy-ex.pdf); [Orient Futures SG](https://www.orientfutures.com.sg/post-detail/Access-China-INE) (snippet)
- OI status is granted per exchange: a broker "must hold formal Overseas Intermediary registration on each individual exchange it wishes to facilitate". — [Orient Futures SG broker-selection guide](https://www.orientfutures.com.sg/china-market-access/how-institutional-traders-select-china-futures-broker/) (snippet)
- How an OI works: it does not trade on the exchange itself but authorizes a futures-company member to trade on its behalf. Nanhua's OI onboarding checklist includes a VM set up for the CTP trading system and **minimum net capital of RMB 30 million** (or the foreign-currency equivalent) for the OI. — [Nanhua "Inbound One-Stop Solution: Qualified Overseas Intermediary", Mar 2022](https://www.nanhua.net/en/download/One-Stop%20Solution%20Overseas%20Intermediary%20202202%20(2022.03).pdf) (snippet)
- OI route compared with QFI: the OI route covers internationalised commodity products, while QFI covers the "full domestic futures and options universe, including financial derivatives" (CFFEX). — [Orient Futures SG](https://www.orientfutures.com.sg/china-market-access/internationalised-vs-qfi-china-futures-access/) (snippet)
- DCE's March 2025 PFMI disclosure lists Overseas Intermediaries and Overseas Special Participants as categories of clearing participant, but gives no counts. — [DCE PFMI Disclosure, Mar 2025](http://www.dce.com.cn/DCE/resource/cms/article/6164698/8632603/DCE-Principles%20for%20Financial%20Market%20Infrastructures%20Disclosure-2024.pdf)
- Scale (dated): in June 2022 INE had **75 registered OIs from 10 countries/regions**, and its overseas clients came from more than 20 countries and regions. — [Xinhua Silk Road, 2022](https://en.imsilkroad.com/p/328548.html)
- A March 2025 BANDS deck says the OI mechanism was then available on INE, DCE and ZCE. — [BANDS webinar deck, Mar 2025](https://bands.financial/files/BANDS%20Webinar%20-%20Trading%20Commodity%20Futures%20in%20China.pdf). SHFE changed its rules from **8 Aug 2025** to allow wider offshore participation. — [HFW](https://www.hfw.com/insights/the-new-silk-road-for-commodities-is-de-dollarisation-how-we-get-there/); [Mondaq on SHFE internationalisation rules](https://webiis08.mondaq.com/china/commoditiesderivativesstock-exchanges/1650952/internationalization-of-shfe-rules-granting-more-access-to-overseas-participants)
- **GFEX:** lithium carbonate futures and options opened to overseas traders on **3 July 2026**, GFEX's first internationalised product. Access is either direct through domestic futures companies or through entrusted OIs. USD can be posted as margin at the RMB central parity rate (one report cites a 5% haircut). GFEX had added products to the QFI programme in March 2025. — [Caixin, 5 Jul 2026](https://www.caixinglobal.com/2026-07-05/china-opens-lithium-futures-market-to-foreign-traders-102460689.html); [China Daily Nansha, 7 Jul 2026](http://nansha.guangdong.chinadaily.com.cn/2026-07/07/c_1195718.htm); [Global Times](https://www.globaltimes.cn/page/202607/1365068.shtml); [Mining.com](https://www.mining.com/web/china-to-open-lithium-futures-to-overseas-traders-next-month/)

### Inferences
- The OI population is concentrated in Hong Kong and Singapore. Singapore-licensed (MAS) entities such as Orient Futures SG, StoneX SG, Phillip Nova, Straits and Marex are the most practical counterparties for non-Chinese individuals and firms. NRIs resident outside India (e.g. in Singapore or the UAE) would face that broker's local onboarding rules, not LRS.
- Exchange coverage widened in 2025–2026. SHFE OI approvals came after the Aug 2025 rule change, and GFEX opened in Jul 2026. Brokers approved early on INE, DCE and ZCE (2018–2021) may not yet have SHFE or GFEX OI status, so coverage should be checked exchange by exchange.
- Because the RMB 30m net-capital threshold applies to the OI, minimums for clients are set by each broker. No client minimums were published in the sources found.

### Gaps
- The full official OI lists from INE, DCE, ZCE, SHFE and GFEX could not be retrieved (DNS blocked). Allowing shfe.com.cn, ine.cn, dce.com.cn, czce.com.cn and gfex.com.cn in network settings would let them be fetched.
- OI status was not verified for GTJA, Haitong, Galaxy, Yongan, GF, CICC, BOCI and others, or for bulge-bracket banks. Banks likely act through QFI, but that is unsourced.
- IBKR and Saxo product availability is unconfirmed.
- Client minimums and retail eligibility were not published.
- No India- or GIFT-City-specific China futures broker was found.

## Platforms: Which trading technology platforms support Chinese futures exchanges?

### Takeaway
CQG and Trading Technologies are the two Western ISVs with a documented, broker-backed China network. CQG has a Shanghai data center, connects to the five major exchanges, and integrates with CTP and Esunny. TT has eight or more mainland and offshore broker partners plus Esunny. Bloomberg (EMSX/FIX through brokers, with hardware in China), FlexTrade (lists DCE, SHFE, ZCE and CFFEX) and Horizon (native CTP, Hundsun and Feichuang) also have documented support. Foreign platforms reach Chinese exchanges through the local counter systems, mainly SFIT CTP and Esunny, run by a mainland futures-company member. I found no public evidence of China connectivity for Stellar, ION Patsystems/Fidessa or Rithmic, and no 2024–2026 TT or CQG GFEX announcement.

### Inferences
- The typical connection chain is: foreign front end (TT, CQG, Bloomberg) → the ISV's China/Shanghai hosting → a mainland futures-company member's CTP or Esunny counter → the exchange, with the OI or foreign client's account sitting at the member. A foreign platform can therefore only offer a China route where a partner futures company supports it.
- GFEX (opened Jul 2026) and SHFE OI flows probably run over the same CTP and Esunny counters, so ISVs already on CTP may support them without a formal announcement. This is unconfirmed.
- Retail-oriented Chinese tools (Wenhua, Shinny/TianQin TqSdk, Kingstar, Quantdo) connect through CTP and are used mainly by onshore clients. I found no sources on their offshore use.

### Gaps
- No sourced information was found on Kingstar, Shinny/TianQin, Wenhua, Fucheng/Quantdo, CTP Mini, Orc/Itiviti, Vela/Exegy, MultiCharts, Quantower, Sierra Chart or ATP.
- Exact exchange coverage on Stellar and Patsystems is unknown; the Orient Futures table needs a direct fetch.
- No 2024–2026 TT or CQG GFEX announcement was found.
- Market data: I found no specifics on China futures data licensing through Bloomberg, LSEG, Wind or CQG, beyond Bloomberg's brochure and CQG's Shanghai data center.
- The Broadridge–CQG acquisition is unverified.
